Where things stand

The Local Plan, the Green Belt, and the proposed care home

August 2026 · please read and confirm by Friday

The short version. The Council's own figures show that 40.3% of the old Horton hospital estate is proposed to come out of the Green Belt. Every part of it has been assessed separately. We have not been able to find anywhere that anyone has added it up.

Two things are happening

They're easy to confuse, and it matters, because different people decide them at different times.

1. The Local Plan

The Council's blueprint for where development goes over the next fifteen years. It proposes taking our surrounding Green Belt land out of protection. A government Planning Inspector is examining it now.

2. The care home application

A live application (reference 26/00002/FUL) for a three-storey, 70-bed care home on the land immediately south of Oak Glade, operating twenty-four hours a day. The Council's planning department decides this one.

There is also HOR004 — around fifty houses proposed on the land to the east, next to Cuddington Glade. No application yet, but it is in the Local Plan.

The two are linked. While the land stays in the Green Belt, a care home there is "inappropriate development" and the applicant has to clear a very high bar. If the Local Plan takes the land out of the Green Belt, that bar disappears. That is why the Local Plan matters most, and why it is happening first.

The strongest point we have

Across the whole historic Horton estate — 204 hectares, five former hospitals — the Local Plan proposes to remove 82 hectares from the Green Belt.

HOR001 and HOR004 are on top of that figure.

Every one of those sites has been assessed on its own, in its own document. Not one of them adds the others up. We have been through the entire published evidence base and cannot find a single assessment of what all of this does to the area as a whole.

And the Council saw it coming. In December 2024 its own officers concluded that neither of the two sites next to us justified being taken out of the Green Belt, and warned in writing about the "slow ebbing away of small areas of Green Belt". Eighteen months later the same Council proposes to release both. The sites haven't changed. National policy has.

We are not saying the Council can't change its mind. We are asking it to show its working.

We have asked — twice

At the Examination hearing on 2 July the Council said the sites had been reassessed using a "revised methodology". We asked where that methodology is written down. The reply described the process but did not identify the document. We asked again what was actually wrong with the original assessments of our two sites. We have had no answer.

There is also a test the Inspector herself set. She required the Council to show that, before releasing Green Belt, it had given first consideration to land already built on or well served by public transport. The care home site is neither — it has never been built on, and it is a twenty-minute walk to the station. We have asked what was considered first. That is unanswered too.

What we've found on the care home

  • The parking doesn't add up. A 70-bed home needs somewhere between 51 and 59 spaces under county standards, rising to 60–70 at shift changeover when two shifts overlap. The application proposes twenty on site, plus eight elsewhere.
  • Our parking spaces aren't a communal car park. Many of us found our bays are written into our individual title deeds — sold with the house. The eight spaces the application relies on are a separate block at the far north end by Cuddington Glade, which means driving the whole length of our private road to reach them.
  • There's a planning condition on our parking. Condition 9 of the 2014 permission that created Oak Glade says the parking area "shall be used and retained exclusively for its designated use" — residential parking for the fifteen houses. That is the Council's own condition, so it is the Council's own question to answer.
  • Our road is private. About 5.5 metres wide. We own it and we pay to maintain it. It was built for twenty-eight households — not for use by the care home's staff, and later fifty more houses at HOR004.

There are further points about rights of way and covenants which we are taking proper advice on before relying on them. They are recorded neutrally in the full review.

Being straight with you about the odds

This is a genuine fight, not a formality.

National planning policy changed significantly last year and now pushes hard for housing and for care provision. Councils are under real pressure. The developer is well resourced and does this professionally.

We may not stop the Green Belt release. If we don't, the care home becomes much harder to resist.

What we can do is make sure the decision is taken with the full picture in front of it, and make it uncomfortable to approve without answering the questions we've raised. That is a realistic aim, and it is worth the effort.
