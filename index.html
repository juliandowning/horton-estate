Where things stand

The Local Plan, the Green Belt, and the proposed care home

Updated 16 October 2026 · no action needed from you today

The short version. The letters went out in August. The Council has now answered one of our questions in writing, and the answer helps us. Two dates are coming: a public consultation on the Local Plan, opening within weeks, and the care home decision in November. Nothing is settled.

What has happened since August

  • 7 August Letters sent to the Planning Inspector and to our two ward councillors, in the joint names of both management companies. Thank you to everyone who read them and came back.
  • August The Programme Officer confirmed the Inspector is not accepting written material at this stage — but that there would be a further, formal opportunity to make the same points at a public consultation on the proposed changes to the plan.
  • 16 September The Council answered our outstanding question in writing, under a formal Environmental Information request. More on what it said below.
  • Late September The care home item, expected at the September planning committee, was put back to November.
  • 15 October The Inspector finalised the schedule of proposed changes to the Local Plan, ready for public consultation. The consultation has not opened yet.

Our two ward councillors have not replied to either letter. A third councillor has been in touch by telephone and has been helpful.

Two things are happening

They're easy to confuse, and it matters, because different people decide them at different times.

1. The Local Plan

The Council's blueprint for where development goes over the next fifteen years. It proposes taking our surrounding Green Belt land out of protection. A government Planning Inspector is examining it, and has now set out the changes she is minded to make.

2. The care home application

A live application (reference 26/00002/FUL) for a three-storey, 70-bed care home on the land immediately south of Oak Glade, operating twenty-four hours a day. The Council's planning committee decides this one, now expected in November.

There is also HOR004 — around fifty houses proposed on the land to the east, next to Cuddington Glade. No application yet, but it is in the Local Plan.

The two are linked. While the land stays in the Green Belt, a care home there is "inappropriate development" and the applicant has to clear a high bar. If the Local Plan takes the land out of the Green Belt, that bar falls away.

And the link runs both ways. The Council's own April 2026 report, which recommends the site for allocation, uses the care home application as part of its reasoning — taking the 70 beds as the expected yield, and citing "the nature of the proposed use for a care home" as a reason to discount the risk to the neighbouring Site of Special Scientific Interest. Each document leans on the other, while neither is settled.

Across the whole historic Horton estate — 204 hectares, five former hospitals — the Local Plan proposes to remove 82 hectares from the Green Belt.

Every one of those sites has been assessed on its own, in its own document. Not one of them adds the others up. We have been through the entire published evidence base and cannot find a single assessment of what all of this does to the area as a whole.

That is the one thing our letter to the Inspector asks for. Not refusal — assessment.

The Council changed its mind, and hasn't said why

In December 2024 the Council's own Green Belt Topic Paper concluded, of both the sites next to us, that each "does not exhibit Exceptional Circumstances justifying an amendment to the Green Belt boundary". Of the care home site it warned in writing about "the slow ebbing away of small areas of Green Belt".

In January 2026 — thirteen months later — the same Council, applying the same two tests, under the same version of national policy, concluded that both sites pass.

We asked, in writing, what changed. The Council's answer of 16 September was that the difference arises because the sites were reassessed "against a revised methodology".

But the Council's own note to the Inspector, written in October 2025, says the review was to be carried out "strictly in accordance with the methodology contained in TP02" — the December 2024 paper. The Inspector's own letter says the same. That note also contains the Council's promise that each conclusion would "clearly state the reasons why exceptional circumstances can (or cannot) be demonstrated for each site."

For our two sites, those reasons have still not been given.

And a comparison worth seeing

In the same January 2026 document, the Council assessed another site — Hollywood Lodge, elsewhere in the borough — and turned it down. The stated reason, in the Council's own summary table, was that releasing it "would create a detached element/satellite within the GB".

That is word for word the finding the Council also recorded about the care home site. One failed on it. One passed.

National policy says that where Green Belt has to be released, priority goes first to land already built on. Here the outcomes run the other way.

In fairness, the Council does draw a distinction: the two sites sit in different Green Belt parcels, and the parcel containing Hollywood Lodge scores more highly overall. That is a real difference and we expect it to be the answer. What the Council has not explained is why the same identified harm is fatal in one case and fixable in the other.

What else the Council's documents show

  • The site got five times bigger on paper. In December 2024 the Council assessed the care home site as suitable for 10 to 15 homes, and said its size and constraints "would severely limit the quantum of development". Sixteen months later the same Council's expected yield for the same site is 70 units.
  • "No significant environmental constraints." That is the Council's April 2026 conclusion on the care home site. The same page lists the constraints as: Green Belt, multiple protected trees on the eastern side, a further protected tree to the south, a boundary within 50 metres of the Epsom Common Site of Special Scientific Interest, groundwater flooding across the whole site, and six recorded sewer flooding incidents in the postcode.
  • The transport score is less impressive than it sounds. The Council says the site "performs well" for accessibility, scoring 18. That is 3rd out of 26 Green Belt sites — but 131st out of 191 sites overall. Only the first figure was quoted to us.
  • The Council has now confirmed in writing that the care home site is not previously developed land. It has never been built on.

The care home and our private road

  • The parking doesn't add up. A 70-bed home needs somewhere between 51 and 59 spaces under county standards, rising to 60–70 at shift changeover when two shifts overlap. The application proposes twenty on site, plus eight more at the far north end of our estate.
  • Those eight spaces are a long way from the site. They sit in a separate block up by Cuddington Glade, which means staff driving the whole length of our private road to reach them. They are in the landowner's ownership, and that is not in dispute — but ownership of a parking space and the right to drive across someone else's road to reach it for a particular purpose are two different things. We are taking proper advice on that before relying on it.
  • Our own bays are in our deeds. Many of us have found our parking spaces written into our individual titles, sold with the house. The estate car park is not a communal resource.
  • There's a planning condition on our parking. Condition 9 of the 2014 permission that created Oak Glade says the parking area "shall be used and retained exclusively for its designated use". That is the Council's own condition, so it is the Council's own question to answer.
  • Our road is private. About 5.5 metres wide, narrowing to 4 metres at the far end. We own it and we pay to maintain it. It serves twenty-eight households across the two estates — not a care home's staff, and not fifty more houses at HOR004 on top.

There are further points about rights of way, covenants and drainage which we are taking proper advice on before relying on them. They are recorded neutrally in the full review.

What happens next

There is another chance to be heard, and it is coming shortly.

On 15 October the Inspector finalised her schedule of proposed changes to the Local Plan. Once the Council publishes it, there will be a public consultation lasting at least six weeks, open to anyone. We will be submitting to it, and the full Planning Evidence Review goes in with it.

Nothing is decided until that consultation has closed, the Inspector has considered every response, and she has issued her final report.

  1. The Local Plan consultation — opening within weeks. We will let you know the moment it does, and we will circulate what we are submitting so you can add your own response if you want to.
  2. The care home committee, November. We can attend and speak. The officers' report is normally published about a week beforehand, and that is the first sight anyone gets of what the Council is actually recommending.
  3. HOR004 — the fifty houses. No application yet, and none expected until the Local Plan is settled.

Worth noting: the care home committee in November will sit before the Local Plan consultation closes. So when the Council decides the care home, the land will still be Green Belt and the proposed allocation will still be provisional.

Being straight with you about the odds

This is a genuine fight, not a formality.

The Inspector has recorded that the borough is roughly 5,500 homes short of what it needs, and that the Green Belt sites in the plan deliver about 1,580 between them. The pressure to find more land is enormous, and it is the reason this review happened at all. The developer is well resourced and does this professionally.

We may not stop the Green Belt release. If we don't, the care home becomes much harder to resist.

What we can do is make sure the decision is taken with the full picture in front of it, and make it uncomfortable to approve without answering the questions we've raised. Everything above comes from the Council's own documents. That is a realistic aim, and it is worth the effort.
