How the consultation works — and what actually counts

This is the part worth reading carefully, because a response that misses it may be set aside without ever reaching the Inspector.

1. Only the modifications are in scope

The Council's report is explicit: representations can be made "only on the proposed Main Modifications to the Local Plan, not the Plan as submitted", and the consultation is "not an opportunity to repeat or raise further representations about the Submission Local Plan."

In practice: a response about MM56, MM57, the policies maps or the Sustainability Appraisal Addendum is in scope. A general objection to building on the Green Belt is not.

2. The test is "soundness", not whether we like it

The Inspector is deciding whether the plan is positively prepared, justified, effective and consistent with national policy. A response that explains why a modification fails one of those four tests carries weight. One that says "we object" does not.

3. Numbers matter less than you would think

A thousand identical emails count for very little. A hundred people each making a real point in their own words count for a great deal. Please do not copy and paste ours — use it for the references and the facts, then say it your own way.

What we have found

We have now read the whole pack. Five things stand out, and all of them come from the Council's and the Inspector's own documents.

  • The plan does not need the care home site. On the Council's own arithmetic the five-year housing supply stands at 5.49 years. Remove the care home site's 37 homes and it is 5.41 years — still comfortably above the five years required. There are 228 homes of headroom.
  • The undetermined application is being counted in the site's favour. The Sustainability Appraisal says of the care home site: "Cuddington Glade is currently the subject of a planning application for a care home, which is considered to be a positive." The application has not been decided. Its merits have not been tested. Meanwhile the policy itself has been written around it — "at least 70 bedrooms", with no upper limit.
  • The environmental assessment of both sites is one sentence. On landscape, the Sustainability Appraisal says: "The two proposed Green Belt allocations to the west are small sites that give rise to limited concerns." On transport it says the same thing. That is the entirety of it. Larger sites elsewhere get several paragraphs each.
  • The documents disagree with each other. The land off Cuddington Glade is a minimum of 45 dwellings in the policy and in the housing trajectory — but "at least 50" in the Council's own monitoring indicator, and 50 in the Sustainability Appraisal. Three documents in one consultation pack, two different numbers.
  • Other sites were dropped, so these were not inevitable. Land west of Burgh Heath Road (50 homes) and the Noble Park extension (90 homes) were both assessed earlier this year and are not in the modifications. The filter exists. It simply did not catch these two.

The care home application

Separate from the Local Plan, and still live. We had been told to expect it at the November planning committee, where residents can register to speak.

One complication we are checking. New national rules take effect on 31 October which change how councils decide which applications go to a committee at all. Under them, an application of this kind is presumed to be decided by officers, and only reaches committee if two named people both agree to refer it. The government's own guidance says referral should be for exceptional circumstances rather than routine.

The Council's report acknowledges the change "will reduce the instances of residents attending Planning Committee to make verbal representations on applications of interest to them."

We are seeking confirmation that our application has been referred, and we will tell you as soon as we know. If it is going to committee, we will circulate the date and the deadline for registering to speak.

What happens when

  1. Monday 26 October — Full Council asked to approve the consultation.
  2. From around 2 November — the six-week consultation opens. We circulate our submission and a short guide. This is the moment to respond.
  3. Monday 14 December — consultation closes.
  4. November — care home application expected at planning committee, subject to the point above.
  5. After that — the Inspector considers every response and issues her final report. Nothing is confirmed until she does.

Being straight with you about the odds

These are the Inspector's own proposed modifications. She has already formed a view that both allocations are needed to make the plan sound. We are asking her to revisit that, which is a harder ask than persuading the Council would have been.

Set against that, the consultation is a statutory stage, every response must be considered, and we have found real problems in the supporting documents — including arithmetic that shows the care home site is not needed to meet the five-year target it is said to serve.

We may not succeed. But this is the one point in the whole process where what residents say has to be read, and it is worth doing properly.
